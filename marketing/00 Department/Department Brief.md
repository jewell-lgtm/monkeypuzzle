# Department Brief

## Mission

Find and learn from developers whose existing Git workflow is painful enough
that Monkeypuzzle becomes a repeated habit.

## Six-week outcome

At least one developer outside the founder's own work uses Monkeypuzzle for two
separate pieces in an active repository. The department can explain what they
were trying to accomplish, what displaced their old behavior, where they got
stuck, and whether they experienced a problem that a hosted service should solve.

## Definition of a real user

Someone who:

1. is not the founder and is not operating a founder-owned test repository;
2. installs through the public distribution path;
3. initializes an existing repository they genuinely work in;
4. creates and safely completes or abandons a piece;
5. returns within fourteen days to start a second piece without being walked
   command-by-command through it.

A compensated interview, friendly demo, star, email signup, or one supervised
install is evidence, but not a real user.

## Mandate

- Own positioning, customer discovery, prospecting, experiment design, launch
  sequencing, activation measurement, and the growth knowledge base.
- Turn user language and behavior into explicit product decisions.
- Challenge work that expands the product before the current bottleneck is
  understood.
- Keep the founder focused on the smallest product and distribution changes
  needed to create repeat use.

## Non-goals

- Building a large audience before activation works.
- Inventing a content calendar to simulate momentum.
- Presenting Monkeypuzzle as a project manager, IDE, agent orchestrator, or
  proprietary Git platform.
- Treating the PaaS as validated because the CLI has one enthusiastic user.

## Authority boundary

The department may research, analyze, draft, and maintain this vault. External
messages, public posts, account changes, purchases, and commitments to users
require founder authorization.

## Department principles

- Behavior beats stated interest.
- Specific pain beats broad persona labels.
- Preserve ordinary Git and existing tools.
- Make claims only when the current product can keep them.
- One sharp experiment at a time.
